# Albert S. Hyman — Heart Clock / Artificial Pacemaker Research
Updated: 2026-11-16

## Primary sources
- **US Patent 1,913,595 A — “Artificial pace maker for the heart”** (Charles Henry Hyman and Albert S. Hyman). Filed March 12, 1930; granted June 13, 1933. https://patents.google.com/patent/US1913595A/en
- **Original patent PDF and illustrations:** https://patentimages.storage.googleapis.com/dc/1b/43/c68ea61f261b09/US1913595.pdf
- **Albert S. Hyman (1932), “Resuscitation of the Stopped Heart by Intracardial Therapy: II. Experimental Use of an Artificial Pacemaker,” _Archives of Internal Medicine_ 50(2):283–305.** https://doi.org/10.1001/archinte.1932.00150150115012
- **Historical review:** https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3232561/ (further reading; evaluate exact relevance)

## Verified mechanism
The patent describes a spring motor, current generator, adjustable rotary interrupter with conductive and insulated disc segments and brushes, a signal lamp, and an electrode needle. It was designed to deliver electrical impulses to a stopped heart in attempted resuscitation, not to detect a person's electromagnetic “signature.”

- No demonstrated ability to locate living or deceased individuals or communicate with the dead through magnetometers.
- A historical pacing apparatus is **not** a safe DIY device to apply electric current to the body. Do not replicate its intracardiac electrical stimulation.
