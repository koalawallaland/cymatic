Light as devotion · 光明燈

In many Taiwanese temples, people dedicate lamps as devotional offerings while praying for peace, clarity, or blessings. Incense and flowers can also express reverence. Practices and meanings vary by temple and tradition.

Here, the digital light and offering are invitations to reflection, not a replacement for an actual temple ritual. This artwork is an independent interpretation, not an official representation of 金山岩.

Taiwan Folklore Dictionary — 光明燈 ↗

A shrine in the mountain · 九份金山岩

The original 金山岩 shrine dates to 1917 and is associated with a rock-carved Guanyin (觀世音菩薩), the bodhisattva of compassion. Its mountainside setting and the artist’s recorded memories form the foundation of this space.

Taiwan National Cultural Memory Bank — 金山岩 ↗

The archival images and edited, silent clips are drawn from the artist’s Jiufen recordings. The pink altar is an original, independent digital design.
