Source notes

GATE and gifted education

What did particular schools ask children to do? These sources identify a district, a classroom account and an interview. The Army’s separate paper describes how military intelligence used Monroe training. A link between the school exercises and that training remains unverified.

  • October 1997

    The Hillsborough clipping

    Pages 1–2 of Education Reporter describe a fourth-grade gifted class in New Jersey. The article quotes instructions for guided imagery, keeping a dream record and designing experiments about purported psychic phenomena. The article is the source available here. The district’s teacher guide would allow a closer check.

  • 1999–2000

    A Las Vegas student’s recollection

    A former student describes attending GATE at Elaine Wynn Elementary. The post identifies the school and years. The associated school forms still need independent verification.

  • January 2025

    Jake Barber’s interview

    Barber recalls one-on-one games and creative activities during school pullouts. Later he says he was tested for psychic abilities. The interview preserves his account. Independent records would be needed to establish who conducted that testing and when.

  • January 1984

    The Army’s Monroe information paper

    This briefing names the military program, CENTER LANE, and describes its use of Hemi-Sync. Its scope is Army intelligence training.

  • Fiction

    The GATE Investigation

    Creator Dan Lawless identifies the apparent investigation as an in-universe companion to his six-part sci-fi series. Annex Research Group and Formulation C-7 belong to that story.

Field notes · 26 November 2026

Tracing the pink-drink account

Which source first puts the drink in a gifted classroom? The historical documents describe three different settings. The proposed connection appears in later accounts.

  • 1934 · Firsthand report

    A card experiment at Duke

    J. B. Rhine describes asking A. J. Linzmayer, a Duke student, to take part in a drug trial during card-guessing tests in 1932. Chapter 5 records his scores and Rhine’s interpretation of them.

    Read Rhine’s account
  • 1966 · Research review

    The passage behind the essay

    K. R. Rao’s “The Effect of Drugs” reviews earlier experiments on substances and scores in tests of purported ESP. The heading appears on PDF page 7 of this declassified book excerpt.

    Open Rao’s review, scan page 7
  • 1955 · Agency draft

    What the drinks document says

    A CIA Chemical Division draft lists research goals. Item 16 mentions giving a substance in drinks. The Senate reproduced it on printed pages 123–124. It identifies neither a school nor a pink beverage.

    Read item 16, printed page 124
  • 1984 · Published account

    A Florida classroom

    A parapsychology quarterly recounts a teacher asking 22 pupils to guess the numbers on cards. The passage on printed page 75 describes the task without identifying a gifted program or mentioning a drink.

    Open the classroom account, page 75
  • June 2026 · Essay

    Where the proposed link appears

    Courtney Buterbaugh gathers recollections of a pink liquid and proposes a connection to drug research. Following her citation leads to Rao’s book. A record connecting those experiments to a named GATE classroom is still missing.

    Read Buterbaugh’s essay
