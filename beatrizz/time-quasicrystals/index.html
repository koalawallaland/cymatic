Chapter 01 · Verified experiment

Order that reappears at another scale

Chen and colleagues (Nature Communications, 22 April 2026) mechanically loaded soft structures based on the Ammann–Beenker tiling. Their local eightfold motifs initially lost their original order, then reorganized into similar larger patterns. Admissible neighboring regions constrain the scales. Chirality and initial void fraction affect whether the patterns form at all.

Chapter 02 · Mathematical model

Temporal order, not time itself

Explore how two frequencies with rational or irrational ratios generate different temporal patterns. For periodic driving, a discrete time crystal can produce a robust collective subharmonic response: T becomes 2T or another integer multiple. Time quasicrystals can produce multiple incommensurate subharmonics. An externally prescribed sinusoid or composite waveform is only an analogy.

s(t) = cos(2πt) + cos(2πrt + α)

For spatial interference, replace time with position and sum waves in directions spaced by 45° to produce an octagonal quasiperiodic pattern. Faraday experiments involve nonlinear wave selection, not merely linear interference. Lifshitz and Petrich · Time quasicrystal experiment.

Chapter 04 · Mathematical audit

What an equation would have to do

There are three different equations, and they must not be conflated.

1 · Confirmed silver-ratio scaling

Lcr / L = 13δN

A ratio of lengths is dimensionless. A real geometric prediction with no gravitational term.

2 · Richard Eskridge's public POAMS proposal

NASA TM 20205010911 reformulates an assumed spin-orbit force while keeping G constant. Its conventional starting terms are Newtonian gravity and centripetal force:

F = Gm₁m₂/r²   ·   F = mv²/r

These two established formulas are not the hypothesized force law. The report's reformulation and assumption set are documented in sections 4–6 and appendix A. The central proposal in equation 28a is shown below. Define A = (mvₒ²/rₒ)(sₚωₚ/rₒ), with sₚ = Iₚωₚ for a classical rotor. Both factors have units of force; A has units of force squared. The sign distinguishes co-spin and anti-spin in the assumed model. No measured coupling from a quasicrystal is provided; the authors describe the experiment as preliminary. Read the actual equations in the report.

Δf = √(FN² ± A) − FN
Equation 28a · assumed model, not established law

In the anti-spin case the radicand can become negative. Equation 28b stipulates Δf = −√(A − FN²) − FN beyond that boundary. This piecewise extension is a model choice, not a physical derivation; the function is continuous there but, as a function of A, its derivative diverges at A = FN². It does not justify a new force. Equation 31 substitutes a dot product of cross-product terms for the signed spin term; a negative radicand remains a concern without the additional branch, and a quantity named as an absolute magnitude can become negative.

Critical assumptions: the original theory treats signed spin kinetic energy as modifying orbital motion, then applies that relation to body-centered forces and intrinsic quantum spin. Ordinary kinetic energy is nonnegative regardless of direction, and intrinsic nuclear spin is not a classical rotating sphere. A new interaction requires an independently motivated Hamiltonian or covariant action, not just this sign choice. The report itself notes a possible superluminal classical nuclear surface-speed calculation.

Experimental audit: section 9 records a scale reading that changed in both directions after mechanical reconfiguration, diminished with repeated trials, and returned following magnet replacement; the authors considered non-gravitational artifacts. Later V5 data remained with Quantum Machines. The 2024 Alzofon replication independently found a thermal artifact; it was not a direct replication of the POAMS rotor. No established gravity modification follows from either record.

3 · Missing Amy equation and proposed bridge

UNVERIFIED The equation described in the interview is not available in the material recovered. A placeholder for the research question, not a proposed discovery, is Δg = η × P × Q, where Δg has acceleration units, P is normalized spin polarization, Q is a dimensionless, independently defined order parameter, and η must have acceleration units. No value or physical justification for η is known. This is dimensional bookkeeping, not Amy's formula, not derived from POAMS, not a prediction.

Δg = η P Q   (hypothetical placeholder)

Before interpreting a phase transition as gravity modification, define Q and its symmetry, write a covariant action linking it to stress-energy or geometry, derive a force in the weak-field limit, account for energy and momentum, give a quantitative prediction, and design a blinded comparison against a matched non-quasiperiodic control. An effective condensed-matter geometry is not automatically physical spacetime.

For context, ordinary general relativity couples spacetime curvature to stress-energy, including pressure and stresses; the Higgs field gives elementary particles mass but most nucleon mass comes from QCD dynamics. A phase change can alter ordinary internal energy and thus its tiny mass equivalent, Δm = ΔE/c². Any novel, much larger gravitational response needs new evidence. CERN: the mass distinction · MICROSCOPE bounds · Effective torsional geometry in Weyl matter.

Strong counterweight: a 2024 independent Alzofon-style experiment found the apparent weight signal was thermal. No verified evidence presently connects any proposed spin coupling to a gravity-modifying quasicrystalline material.

Paul J. Steinhardt — The Second Kind of Impossible (2019)

The origin of quasicrystal theory and the hunt for natural specimens. Read for the interplay between an unconventional mathematical idea and the painstaking experimental and geological evidence that could establish it..

Christian Janot — Quasicrystals: A Primer (2nd edition)

Technical foundation: diffraction, tilings, reciprocal-space indexing, higher-dimensional descriptions, structure and physical properties. Read while studying the Fibonacci and Pell recurrences, projection windows, and phonons/phasons.

Symmetry, geometry and physical law; useful background for understanding why symmetry breaking yields new kinds of order and how mathematical aesthetics differ from empirical confirmation.
